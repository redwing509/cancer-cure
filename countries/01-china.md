# China

**This is not medical advice.** Nothing in this note is a recommendation to start, stop, or replace cancer treatment. No entry is a “cure” unless a cited, opened source reports a cure in those words, and even then the entry is a record of that source, not medical advice. Talk to an oncologist before changing care. Read the [repository disclaimer](../README.md) and [`METHOD.md`](../METHOD.md).

Country: People's Republic of China. First country note in this series. Traditional Chinese medicine has a large published trial literature; this note records what opened sources actually measured, including null results, limitations, and retractions.

**Per-case rule.** Cancers are not always caused by one thing in particular, and a cancer in one area of the body can have a different cause or a different response to a therapy than a cancer in another area. Every finding below is treated on its own. Each study entry names its **cancer type** and **body site** as the opened source uses those terms. A hepatocellular carcinoma result is not an acute promyelocytic leukemia result. Mixed-type studies and program pages are labeled as such, not collapsed into a general cancer finding. See the per-case rule in [`METHOD.md`](../METHOD.md).

## How this note was prepared

A Hermes research draft (job `20261119-130857-cancer-cure-survey-01-china-natural-and-`, OVH DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, profile `we-research`) collected candidate sources. Every finding below was then citation-checked by a human-supervised pass against pages or abstracts opened for this check. Quotes are copied from those opened sources. Names, numbers, and intervals that could not be confirmed were dropped or corrected. This file is not a dump of the draft.

**Checker corrections and scope notes:**

- **Realgar–Indigo (RIF) versus arsenic trioxide (S03).** The paper’s intention-to-treat 2-year disease-free survival difference is **−1% (95% CI −4.8 to 6.9)**. A draft interval of −4.9 to 7.0 is the **per-protocol** interval in the same paper, not the ITT result. Both are quoted below.
- **Huaier (S01)** is an open-label (unblinded) multicenter randomized trial; patients and physicians knew allocation. The comparison is adjuvant Huaier versus no additional intervention after curative liver resection.
- **Chao 2016 bicarbonate TACE (S10)** and **Jin 2025 TILA-TACE (S11)** have openly available full text. The draft had labeled both abstract-only; this note labels them as full text. Chao is a small pilot (nonrandomized plus a 20-patient randomized group). Jin is a real-world series, not a randomized trial.
- **Ginseng fatigue meta-analysis (S06).** The same abstract reports a **null emotional-fatigue** result (SMD 0.12, *P* = .15). PMID is 36401389 (PMC9678550). The PMC HTML could not be fetched from this environment (HTTP 403); quotes are from the opened journal/PubMed abstract.
- **Acupuncture analgesic-use sentence (S09)** was confirmed in the **full text**, not the abstract: “There was no statistically significant difference in analgesic use among the three study arms (P > 0.05).” The trial enrolled 30 patients on the Mainland and 12 in Hong Kong.
- **A 2026 *International Journal of General Medicine* review** cited in the draft (PMC13770382 / 10.2147/IJGM.S631752) could not be opened here (publisher bot-protection). **No quotes from that paper are used.**
- **A 2016 *People’s Daily* health item** on baking-soda coverage (S12) could not be opened here (HTTP 500). **No quotes from that page are used.**
- **Cinobufacini (S28)** is included because the opened abstract reports a **null 6-month survival** comparison and rates most included trials as low quality.
- Programs led or funded by the U.S. National Cancer Institute are labeled as such (Linxian nutrition trials; the opened Xuanwei case-control abstract is a collaboration reported in *International Journal of Cancer*).
- This note does **not** conclude that cancer is a fungus. Bicarbonate TACE papers below describe an arterial-infusion technique; they are not evidence that cancer is a fungal infection.
- **Per-case fields (t1201u, 19 Nov 2026).** Cancer type and body site were added to each finding in this revision. The values are taken from sources already opened for the original check; where a source does not name a type or site, the field says so and nothing is inferred.

## Natural and traditional therapies

### Huaier granule (adjuvant after curative resection of HCC)

- **Compound or therapy:** Huaier granule (a *Trametes robiniophila* Murr. product), adjuvant, after curative resection
- **Cancer type:** Hepatocellular carcinoma, as named in the opened paper
- **Body site:** Liver
- **Country:** China (multicenter)
- **Study type:** “multicentre, randomised, controlled, phase IV trial” (open-label; no additional intervention in the control arm)
- **Population:** 1,044 patients with hepatocellular carcinoma randomized 2:1 after curative liver resection; efficacy analyses reported for 686 Huaier vs 316 no-Huaier in the opened abstract
- **Measured result (quoted):** “The recurrence-free survival (RFS) was 62.39% versus 49.05% in the experimental versus control group (HR 0.67; 95% CI 0.52 to 0.87). Overall survival was 95.19% versus 91.46%, respectively (HR 0.51; 95% CI 0.28 to 0.93). The extrahepatic recurrence rate was 8.60% versus 13.61% (P=0.0255).”
- **Link or DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1136/gutjnl-2018-315983 — Chen et al., *Gut* (2018). Opened on gut.bmj.com (Free*).
- **Evidence level:** RCT
- **Negative / null results:** The trial was open-label. The opened abstract does not report a placebo or blinded comparator. Safety details beyond the survival endpoints were not copied from a full-text adverse-event table in this check.
- **Source opened as:** full text (journal HTML, Free*)

### Compound Kushen injection plus chemotherapy in NSCLC

- **Compound or therapy:** Compound Kushen injection (CKI) plus vinorelbine and cisplatin (NP) or plus gemcitabine and cisplatin (GP), versus NP or GP alone
- **Cancer type:** Non-small-cell lung cancer, as named in the opened paper
- **Body site:** Lung
- **Country:** China (included trials; meta-analysis)
- **Study type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials
- **Population:** 10 studies, 1,019 patients with non-small-cell lung cancer, as stated in the opened paper
- **Measured result (quoted):** “The results indicated that CKI combined with NP or GP had a better objective response rate (relative risk (RR) = 1.21, 95% confidence interval (CI) = 1.06–1.37, P = 0.003).” The conclusion also states improvements in Karnofsky performance and a reduction in adverse reactions (KPS RR = 2.18; MD = 0.82; AE RR = 0.67 in the opened paper).
- **Link or DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1155/2019/7241927 — PMC6875363, *Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine* (2019)
- **Evidence level:** systematic review
- **Negative / null results:** This paper itself was **not** retracted on the pages opened for this check. The same journal later issued publisher-level retractions of other articles for systematic manipulation of peer review (see Retractions). Treat the CKI meta-analysis with that journal-level caution; it is not independent high-quality evidence.
- **Source opened as:** full text (PMC)

### Realgar–Indigo naturalis formula versus arsenic trioxide in non-high-risk APL

- **Compound or therapy:** All-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) plus oral Realgar–Indigo naturalis formula (RIF) versus ATRA plus intravenous arsenic trioxide (ATO), after ATRA-ATO induction, as post-remission therapy
- **Cancer type:** Non-high-risk acute promyelocytic leukemia, as named in the opened paper
- **Body site:** Blood and bone marrow
- **Country:** China (multicenter)
- **Study type:** Multicenter randomized controlled non-inferiority trial (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02899169), reported in *Haematologica* (2025)
- **Population:** 108 eligible patients with non-high-risk acute promyelocytic leukemia (56 male, 52 female; median age 40); all achieved hematological complete remission after induction; median follow-up 29 months
- **Measured result (quoted):** “The primary endpoint of 2-year disease-free survival was 97% in the ATRA-RIF arm and 98% in the ATRA-ATO arm, respectively (the ATRA-RIF arm was found to be non-inferior to the ATRA-ATO arm, [P<0.01], with a percentage difference of −1% [95% confidence interval: −4.8 to 6.9]). No deaths have been observed.” In the per-protocol analysis (47 patients per arm who completed treatment as planned): “The percentage difference between the two groups was −1% (95% CI: −4.9 to 7.0), confirming non-inferiority.”
- **Link or DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3324/haematol.2024.285905
- **Evidence level:** RCT
- **Negative / null results:** “Grade 3 to 4 disseminated intravascular coagulation was reported in 14 (14%) patients.” Most adverse events were described as moderate. This trial tests an oral arsenic regimen against intravenous ATO; it is not a study of an unapproved folk remedy.
- **Source opened as:** full text (journal HTML)

### Arsenic trioxide in relapsed acute promyelocytic leukemia (1997)

- **Compound or therapy:** Arsenic trioxide (As₂O₃)
- **Cancer type:** Relapsed acute promyelocytic leukemia, as named in the opened abstract
- **Body site:** Blood and bone marrow
- **Country:** China
- **Study type:** Early clinical study of relapsed APL, as described in the PubMed abstract
- **Population:** 15 patients with relapsed acute promyelocytic leukemia
- **Measured result (quoted):** “Complete remission was achieved in 9 of 10 patients treated with As2O3 alone and in all the rest 5 patients treated with the combination of As2O3 and low-dose chemotherapy or retinoic acid, respectively.”
- **Link or DOI:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9129042/ — PMID 9129042, *Blood* (1997)
- **Evidence level:** early trial
- **Negative / null results:** One of 10 patients treated with As₂O₃ alone did not achieve complete remission on that regimen (implied by “9 of 10”). Adverse-event detail was not copied from a full-text source in this check.
- **Source opened as:** abstract only

### Kanglaite injection plus gemcitabine and cisplatin in NSCLC

- **Compound or therapy:** Kanglaite injection (KLT) plus gemcitabine and cisplatin (GP)

### Ginseng for cancer-related fatigue

- **Compound or therapy:** Ginseng, oral and injectable
- **Cancer type:** Not stated as a single cancer type in the opened abstract. This is a cancer-related-fatigue (symptom) meta-analysis across included trials, not a tumour-control result in one disease
- **Body site:** not stated as a single site in the opened abstract
- **Country:** China (corresponding team at Hebei Province Hospital of Chinese Medicine / Hebei University of Chinese Medicine; included trials from multiple settings)
- **Study type:** Meta-analysis
- **Population:** Twelve studies in the pooled CRF analysis; six oral-administration studies; ten injection studies, as stated in the abstract
- **Measured result (quoted):** “Twelve studies were included to evaluate efficacy of ginseng oral administration and ginseng injections on CRF. The pooled SMD was 0.40 (95% confidence Interval [95% CI] [0.29–0.51], P < .00001).” Conclusion: “Ginseng can improve CRF. Intravenous injection might be better than oral administration. Ginseng injections may alleviate cognitive fatigue. No evidence was found to support that ginseng could alleviate emotional fatigue.”
- **Link or DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000031363 — Li et al., *Medicine* 2022;101(46):e31363. PMID 36401389, PMC9678550
- **Evidence level:** systematic review
- **Negative / null results:** “Emotional fatigue was reported in 4 studies… The pooled SMD was 0.12 (95% CI [−0.04 to 0.29], P = .15).” This is a **symptom** outcome (cancer-related fatigue), not a tumour-response or survival endpoint.
- **Source opened as:** abstract only (journal/PubMed abstract; PMC HTML returned HTTP 403 in this environment)

### Acupuncture at Si Guan Xue for cancer pain (pilot RCT)

- **Compound or therapy:** Acupuncture at Si Guan Xue
- **Cancer type:** Mixed cancer types, as described in the opened paper (not one disease). The paper states that among Mainland participants the majority had a primary lung cancer, and among Hong Kong participants the majority had a head or neck primary; most patients had stage IV disease
- **Body site:** Mixed; lung and head or neck were the most common primaries in the subgroups named above. This entry is not a result for one site only
- **Country:** China, with a Hong Kong site (30 participants on the Mainland, 12 in Hong Kong)
- **Study type:** Pilot randomized trial, three arms, 14 patients per arm
- **Population:** 42 cancer patients with pain (20 men, 22 women; 37 with stage IV disease). 45 were screened; 3 declined. 30 (71.4%) completed the planned course; all 42 were analyzed after at least two sessions.
- **Measured result (quoted):** “The analysis showed that the cancer pain reduction in treatment arm 2 was most prominent on day 5 when compared with the control arm (P<0.05). There was no difference in the scores of PGIC, EORTC QLQ-C30 or KPS among the three groups (P>0.05).” In the body: “Moreover, the use of analgesics was recorded for all accrued patients. There was no statistically significant difference in analgesic use among the three study arms (P > 0.05) (Tables 2 and 3).”
- **Link or DOI:** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5485510/ — *BMC Complementary and Alternative Medicine* (2017)
- **Evidence level:** early trial
- **Negative / null results:** No statistically significant difference in analgesic use, PGIC, EORTC QLQ-C30, or KPS. The authors note the short evaluation period and small sample as possible reasons. No serious adverse events were observed, as stated in the abstract.
- **Source opened as:** full text (PMC)

### Cinobufacini injection in moderate and advanced primary liver cancer

- **Compound or therapy:** Cinobufacini injection (a preparation from *Bufo* toad), alone or plus TACE, versus no cinobufacini or TACE only
- **Cancer type:** Moderate and advanced primary liver cancer, as named in the opened paper
- **Body site:** Liver
- **Country:** China (included trials; meta-analysis). Authors include affiliations in Putian, Zhengzhou, and international co-authors.
- **Study type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials
- **Population:** 11 RCTs, 728 patients with moderate and advanced primary liver cancer
- **Measured result (quoted):** “A total of 11 RCTs consisting of 728 patients were included. All of the trials demonstrated significantly improved total response rates, total response rates of Karnofsky Performance Score (KPS), 1- to 2-year survival rates, and quality of life in the intervention groups injected with cinobufacini. There was no statistically significant difference between the groups (cinobufacini versus no cinobufacini; cinobufacini plus transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) versus TACE only) in terms of the 6-month survival rate, clinical benefit rate and clinical benefit rate of KPS.”
- **Link or DOI:** http://jcps.bjmu.edu.cn/EN/10.5246/jcps.2019.04.027 — Dong et al., *Journal of Chinese Pharmaceutical Sciences* (2019)
- **Evidence level:** systematic review
- **Negative / null results:** No statistically significant difference in **6-month survival**, clinical benefit rate, or clinical benefit rate of KPS. “Given that the majority of the trials were of low quality, more high-quality prospective RCTs with strict design in accordance with CONSORT for TCM are needed.”
- **Source opened as:** full text (journal HTML / abstract page with the results paragraph)

## Bicarbonate with TACE, and the fungus hypothesis

These entries test or describe **bicarbonate as part of a TACE technique**, or describe **fungi found in tumours**. They are filed here so the [cancer-fungus investigation](../investigations/cancer-fungus-hypothesis.md) has China-sourced measurements. **They do not establish that cancer is a fungus**, and they are not a study of oral baking-soda therapy as popularized outside oncology practice. Under the per-case rule, a liver-tumour TACE result or an esophageal or lung mycobiome result is not a finding for cancer in general, or for any other site.

### TACE plus local bicarbonate (Chao et al., 2016)

- **Compound or therapy:** Transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) plus local bicarbonate
- **Cancer type:** Hepatocellular carcinoma / hepatocarcinoma, as named in the opened paper
- **Body site:** Liver
- **Country:** China
- **Study type:** Pilot clinical study with a nonrandomized series and a small randomized comparison
- **Population:** 57 patients in a nonrandomized series and 20 in a randomized comparison, as described in the opened paper
- **Measured result (quoted):** Objective response rate “100% vs 44.4%” in the nonrandomized comparison and “63.6%” in the randomized control arm, as reported in the opened paper (ORR 100% with bicarbonate-containing treatment versus 44.4% nonrandomized control and 63.6% randomized control).
- **Link or DOI:** https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.17536 — PMC4970867, *eLife* (2016)
- **Evidence level:** early trial
- **Negative / null results:** This is a small pilot. The randomized control arm already had a 63.6% ORR. The paper studies an interventional radiology technique, not the claim that cancer is a fungal infection.
- **Source opened as:** full text (PMC)

### TILA-TACE (Jin et al., 2025)

- **Compound or therapy:** Targeting-intratumoral-lactic-acidosis TACE (TILA-TACE), a bicarbonate-containing TACE protocol
- **Cancer type:** Hepatocellular carcinoma, as named in the opened paper
- **Body site:** Liver
- **Country:** China
- **Study type:** Real-world / observational clinical series (ChiCTR-ONC-17013416)
- **Population:** 413 patients; median follow-up 38 months
- **Measured result (quoted):** ORR 99.01%, complete response 72.77%; advanced-stage median survival 27 months, as reported on the opened Nature *Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy* page.
- **Link or DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41392-025-02400-x
- **Evidence level:** observational
- **Negative / null results:** Not a randomized trial. The opened report is a real-world series of an interventional protocol, not a test of oral bicarbonate or of a fungus-cause theory.
- **Source opened as:** full text (open access)

### Esophageal squamous-cell carcinoma mycobiome (Fujian and collaborators)

- **Compound or therapy:** Not a therapy; tumour mycobiome description
- **Cancer type:** Esophageal squamous-cell carcinoma, as named in the opened paper
- **Body site:** Esophagus
- **Country:** China
- **Study type:** Observational mycobiome comparison of tumour vs adjacent tissue
- **Population:** Esophageal squamous-cell carcinoma tumour and tumour-adjacent samples, as described in the opened paper
- **Measured result (quoted):** “The mycobiota diversity was significantly different between tumors and tumor-adjacent samples.” “Unlike in the tumor samples, tumor-adjacent samples showed a significantly higher mycobiota alpha diversity (observed ASVs, P = 0.001; Shannon index, P = 0.013).”
- **Link or DOI:** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10147516/
- **Evidence level:** observational
- **Negative / null results:** A difference in fungal communities between tumour and adjacent tissue is not evidence that cancer is caused by a fungus, and the paper does not treat bicarbonate as a cure.
- **Source opened as:** full text (PMC)

### Lung mycobiome in NSCLC

- **Compound or therapy:** Not a therapy; lung mycobiome description
- **Cancer type:** Non-small-cell lung cancer, as named in the opened paper
- **Body site:** Lung
- **Country:** China
- **Study type:** Observational comparison of NSCLC vs non-NSCLC samples
- **Population:** 38 samples in the beta-diversity comparison described in the opened paper
- **Measured result (quoted):** “Furthermore, the fungal communities in patients with NSCLC showed a significantly higher β-diversity ( p = 0.003).”
- **Link or DOI:** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10039514/
- **Evidence level:** observational
- **Negative / null results:** Higher fungal β-diversity in patients with NSCLC is not a demonstration that cancer is a fungus or that antifungal or bicarbonate therapy is effective.
- **Source opened as:** full text (PMC)

## Cause-research programs and etiology studies

### Qidong (hepatitis B, aflatoxin, microcystin)

- **Compound or therapy:** Not a therapy; etiology and prevention research in Qidong, Jiangsu
- **Cancer type:** Liver cancer, as named in the opened reviews quoted below
- **Body site:** Liver
- **Country:** China
- **Study type:** Observational / molecular epidemiologic synthesis
- **Population:** Qidong residents in an historically high-incidence liver-cancer area, with a cancer registry and Liver Cancer Institute established in 1972 (as described in the 2019 review)
- **Measured result (quoted):** From Chen et al., *Hepatoma Research* (2018): relative risks **13.25** and **28.05** for combined exposures, with the confidence intervals given in that opened paper. From Chen, Zhu, Wang, Groopman and Kensler, *Cancer Biology & Medicine* (2019): “Notably, the China age-standardized incidence rate for liver cancer has dropped by over 50% in the past several decades.” “Molecular epidemiologic and genomic deep sequencing studies have affirmed that infection with hepatitis B virus as well as dietary exposure to aflatoxins through contamination of dietary staples such as corn, and to microcystins – blue-green algal toxins found in ditch and pond water – were likely important etiologic factors that account for the high incidence of liver cancer in this region.”
- **Link or DOI:** https://doi.org/10.20517/2394-5079.2017.50 ; https://www.cancerbiomed.org/content/16/1/24
- **Evidence level:** observational
- **Negative / null results:** not reported in the opened source as a failed intervention; the 2019 review attributes later incidence declines to vaccination, safer water, and dietary change.
- **Source opened as:** full text (both journal pages)

### Linxian nutrition intervention trials (US NCI-led)

- **Compound or therapy:** Multiple vitamin and mineral supplements, including a selenium / vitamin E / beta-carotene combination in the general-population trial
- **Cancer type:** Esophageal cancer and gastric cardia cancer, as named on the opened NCI page (two types; not a single “cancer” result)
- **Body site:** Esophagus and gastric cardia. Endpoints quoted on that page also include total mortality, total cancer mortality, and gastric cancer mortality, and those are not collapsed into one site here
- **Country:** China (Linxian); **led and funded by the U.S. National Cancer Institute** (intramural DCEG), with local implementation
- **Study type:** Two randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled nutrition intervention trials (dysplasia trial, n = 3,318, six years; general-population trial, n = 29,584, 5.25 years)
- **Population:** Adults 40–69 years in northern Linxian communes, an area described on the opened NCI page as having among the highest esophageal/gastric cardia cancer mortality rates in the world
- **Measured result (quoted):** “With the conclusion of the intervention phase in 1991 and determination that the micronutrient combination of selenium/vitamin E/beta-carotene reduced total mortality, total cancer mortality, and gastric cancer mortality, objectives now include determination of post-trial effects of the interventions and further evaluation of etiologic hypotheses.” The same page records 448 incident cancers in the dysplasia trial and 1,298 in the general-population trial during the intervention.
- **Link or DOI:** https://epi.grants.cancer.gov/cohort-consortium/members/linxian.html (NCI Epidemiology and Genomics Research Program cohort page, last updated 14 Dec 2021 as printed on the opened page)
- **Evidence level:** RCT
- **Negative / null results:** The opened program page emphasizes one nutrient combination that reduced the listed mortality endpoints; it does not, on that page, list every factorial arm that did not. Arms not described there are not treated as findings in this note.
- **Source opened as:** full text (official NCI cohort page)

### Xuanwei smoky-coal lung cancer (case-control)

- **Compound or therapy:** Not a therapy; indoor smoky (bituminous) coal combustion
