# Incidence trends over the last century

**Status:** Outline and method only. The citation-checked evidence review is not in this file yet and will arrive in a later pull request.

**This is not medical advice.** Read the [disclaimer in the README](../README.md).

## Question

Reported cancer counts in many countries are much higher now than they were a century ago. The question is **how much of that change remains after adjusting for age, life expectancy, and diagnosis rates**, and only then which causes might explain a remaining change.

Do not treat “almost nobody had cancer a century ago” as a finding in this file until age-standardized or age-specific sources have been opened and quoted. People also died younger of other causes, and many cancers were not diagnosed or were recorded under other names. Those facts are hypotheses to check, not results.

## What the later evidence PR must do

1. Open official or peer-reviewed series that report **age-standardized or age-specific incidence or mortality**, not crude counts alone. Starting leads to open and check (not findings of this outline) include long-running registries and compendia such as CI5, SEER, or national statistics offices, and papers that reconstruct historical rates. If a named series cannot be opened, write `NOT FOUND`.
2. Quote how each source handles **age structure**, **life expectancy or competing causes of death**, **screening and diagnostic practice**, **registry completeness**, and **coding changes** (for example ICD revisions). If a source does not adjust for one of these, say so.
3. Separate **mortality** from **incidence** when the source does. Better survival and earlier diagnosis can raise incidence while changing mortality in a different direction.
4. Break results out by **cancer type and body site** where the source does. A rise in one site and a fall in another must not be collapsed into a single “cancer is rising” sentence. Follow the per-case rule in [`METHOD.md`](../METHOD.md).
5. After those adjustments, list remaining increases or decreases as questions for the candidate-cause files, with quotes and links. Do not invent a “smoking gun” cause in this note.

## Method

Follow [`METHOD.md`](../METHOD.md), including the per-case rule: two passes, quote plus URL or DOI, `NOT FOUND` rather than a guess, no Sci-Hub or Library Genesis, no unsourced numbers. Both apparent increases and apparent stable or falling rates, after adjustment, are to be reported, by type and site.

Until the evidence PR is checked and merged, this note has **no conclusion**.
