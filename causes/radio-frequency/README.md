## Cancer-causation question

Do **radio-frequency electromagnetic fields (RF-EMF)**, including from wireless communications and from high-power occupational or radar sources, cause cancer in humans, and if so at what exposures and at which sites?

This branch has no preferred answer. The existence of a scientific debate is not itself evidence of causation. Evidence decides. Both **null and positive** measured results are to be quoted.

IARC’s 2011 classification of RF-EMF, often described as Group 2B, is a starting lead to **open and quote** (Monograph 102). A classification is not a substitute for reading the underlying studies, and this outline does not treat Group 2B as proof that RF fields cause cancer, or as proof that they do not.

## Starting sources to open in the evidence PR

These titles are a search list, not findings. Do not copy numbers, confidence intervals, or animal-tumor counts from memory. If a source cannot be opened legitimately, write `NOT FOUND` or “paywalled; no legitimate open copy found.”

1. **IARC Monograph 102** (2011) and the official statement of the RF-EMF classification
2. **NTP** (U.S. National Toxicology Program) RF studies, 2018 technical reports and later related statements
3. **Ramazzini Institute** RF animal study, 2018
4. **Interphone** study publications
5. The **Danish cohort** of mobile-phone subscribers and related follow-up papers
6. The **2024 WHO-commissioned systematic review** associated with **Karipidis et al.**, and any published critiques or responses that can be opened
7. **High-power occupational and radar exposure** studies (for example radar, broadcast, or industrial RF workers), including both positive and null findings

For each opened source, quote the measured result (human epidemiology or animal pathology, as applicable), the exposure description, the population or model, and the authors’ own limitations. Keep phone-use studies distinct from high-power occupational and radar studies.
